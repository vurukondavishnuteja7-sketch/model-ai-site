How to Understand and Calculate CGPA

CGPA is a cumulative grade-point average used by many schools and universities to summarize performance across courses or terms. The exact calculation depends on the institution’s grading scheme, credit weights and published rules. Use this guide to understand the terms and identify the right formula before converting a score.

7 min read677 wordsUpdated 17 October 2026

CGPA, SGPA and credits

CGPA usually refers to a cumulative measure across multiple subjects or semesters. SGPA usually refers to one semester. A course may carry credits, and those credits can represent its relative weight in the result. When credits are used, the average should reflect them instead of treating every course as identical.

A common weighted form is: grade-point average = sum of (course grade point × course credits) ÷ sum of course credits. This gives a course with more credits a larger contribution to the result. However, not every school uses exactly the same scale or rounding rules, so the student handbook or official transcript guidance takes priority.

Illustrative weighted example

Course A: 8 points × 4 credits = 32. Course B: 9 points × 3 credits = 27. Course C: 7 points × 2 credits = 14.

Total weighted points = 73; total credits = 9.

73 ÷ 9 = 8.11 CGPA (illustrative)

This example explains weighting only; use your institution’s rules for an official result.

Why one percentage formula does not fit everyone

A conversion such as CGPA × 9.5 is sometimes associated with particular grading systems, but it is not a universal rule for every university or board. Some institutions publish a different multiplier, some provide a grade-to-percentage table, and others use a formula tied to the course or program.

Before converting, search your official regulations or transcript for wording such as “CGPA to percentage”, “conversion factor”, “equivalence certificate” or “grading scheme”. If a college or employer asks for a percentage, follow the rule they specify rather than selecting a formula from an unrelated website.

Worked conversion example

Suppose your institution explicitly states that percentage equals CGPA multiplied by 10. If your CGPA is 8.2, the conversion under that rule would be 82%. If your institution states a different formula, the result changes. The number 8.2 alone is not enough to determine an official percentage without knowing the applicable grading rule.

Keep the original CGPA on forms that request CGPA. Do not convert it to a percentage unless the form asks for that format or provides an approved conversion method.

Before using a conversion
  1. Identify the institution and program.
  2. Find the official conversion rule for your cohort.
  3. Enter the CGPA at the precision shown on your transcript.
  4. Apply only the published formula, then follow its rounding instruction.

Common CGPA mistakes

One common mistake is multiplying by a familiar factor without checking the rules. Another is using a simple average when semester credit totals are different. Students also sometimes mix grade points with percentages or enter SGPA values into a CGPA field. Confirm whether a calculator expects one semester, each course, or a cumulative transcript.

If a transcript reports “withheld”, “incomplete”, “backlog” or a special status, a calculator cannot decide how the institution treats it. Ask the academic office for clarification.

Use a calculator as a cross-check

A CGPA tool is useful for exploring scenarios and checking arithmetic, but it cannot replace an official grade sheet. Keep a record of course credits and grade points as printed on your transcript. After calculating, compare the result with the student portal or official academic record.

For applications, scholarships and admissions, retain the institution’s conversion policy alongside your result. That makes it easier to explain how a percentage was derived if a reviewer asks.

Frequently asked questions

Is CGPA the same as percentage?

No. CGPA is expressed as grade points under a grading scheme. Percentage is a score out of 100. A conversion is only official when the institution defines the applicable method.

Can I calculate CGPA by averaging semesters?

Only if the grading rules permit that method. If semesters have different credit totals, a simple average can give the wrong result; the official method may use credits or cumulative grade points.

Which formula should I use for applications?

Use the published conversion rule from your school or university, or ask its academic office. Do not assume that one multiplier applies to every institution.
